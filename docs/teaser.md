# New Vesper

It's always raining in New Vesper. Magic and machines both run on the same fuel: *attention*. Whatever the city remembers stays real. Whatever it forgets fades: streets drop off the maps, and people stop being seen.

You start in the Market District, a night market under lanterns so thick the rain comes through orange. Noodles, bootleg firmware and secondhand miracles are sold side by side, prices are called in five languages, and there's a small, damp god of lost umbrellas whose appeal the Registry has lost four times. Under the oldest bazaar hall, a drowned subway station leads down to Old Vesper, the city that was forgotten. A rat with a lantern-boat will ferry you there if you pay in something someone remembers.

You're not a class. You're a street kid who knows someone in every market, or a made person who quit selling subscription prayers, or a cat nobody suspects. An AI dungeon master tells the story, but the dice and the rules are code it can't sweet-talk. The dice can never kill you: if you die, it's because you chose to go out doing something that changes the city. The world is shared too, so keeping a shrine lit or a street remembered keeps it real for everyone who plays.

Grim, bright, a bit ridiculous, and it remembers what you did.
